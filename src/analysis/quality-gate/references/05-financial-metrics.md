# Financial Analysis and Key Metrics

> Trimmed from buffett-skills (github.com/agi-now/buffett-skills) reference 05-financial-metrics.

## Core Principle

GAAP statements are the starting point, not the destination. The question: how much does this business truly earn?

## Owner Earnings

```
Owner Earnings = Net Income + D&A − Maintenance CapEx − required net working-capital increases
```

Maintenance CapEx (must be spent or the competitive position deteriorates) is deducted; growth CapEx is not. EBITDA is unreliable because it implicitly assumes assets never wear out. Estimate maintenance CapEx from management disclosure, or depreciation × industry multiplier (≈1:1 for asset-light, higher for capital-intensive).

## Profitability

**ROE** = Net income / average shareholders' equity. Standard: 10-year average above 15% with no excessive leverage. Decompose the source — ROE inflated by leverage is a trap, not an advantage.

**ROIC** = NOPAT / invested capital (equity + interest-bearing debt − cash). More honest than ROE: removes capital-structure distortion. Above 15% sustained is excellent; above 20% exceptional. Value is created only when ROIC exceeds the cost of capital.

## Cash Flow Quality

**Cash conversion ratio** = operating cash flow / net income. Sustained above 90%: high earnings quality. Persistently below 70%: investigate — possibly inflated receivables or aggressive revenue recognition.

**FCF** = operating cash flow − total CapEx. Includes growth CapEx, so it suits asset-light businesses; use the owner-earnings lens for heavy-CapEx growth companies.

## Balance Sheet Health

- Debt / EBITDA below 2x: comfortable.
- Interest coverage (EBIT / interest expense) above 5x: sufficient buffer.
- Worst-case test (mandatory): assume revenue falls 30% — can the company repay maturing debt, sustain core operations, and avoid dilutive financing?

## Earnings-Quality Red Flags

Investigate further if any appear:
- Receivables growth persistently above revenue growth
- Inventory growth persistently above revenue growth
- Operating cash flow persistently below net income
- Frequent "adjusted earnings" diverging widely from GAAP
- Large goodwill from high-premium acquisitions with mediocre returns
- Frequent non-transparent related-party transactions
- Auditor changes or qualified audit opinions
- Management selling large amounts of shares

## Look-Through Earnings

For holding companies or businesses with large unconsolidated stakes: reported operating earnings + proportionate share of investees' retained earnings − estimated taxes if distributed. GAAP's 20% ownership threshold makes retainee earnings invisible; judge them by how well they are deployed, not by the stake size.
