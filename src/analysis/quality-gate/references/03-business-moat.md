# Business Quality: Moat and Business Model

> Trimmed from buffett-skills (github.com/agi-now/buffett-skills) reference 03-business-moat.

## Five Types of Moats

1. **Intangible assets (brand, patents, licenses).** Brand moat test: pricing power (a candy bar can raise prices; sugar cannot), perennial demand, globalization, consistent quality. Recognition is not a moat — many widely known companies have no pricing power (most airlines).
2. **Low-cost advantage.** Scale (Costco, GEICO), geographic/resource endowment (rail networks), process culture. Key question: could a competitor match your cost structure by spending $1 billion over 5 years?
3. **Switching costs.** Deep data integration, retraining burden, process disruption. Quantitative signal: net revenue retention >120%.
4. **Network effects.** Direct (messaging), indirect/platform (Visa), data-driven (search). Test: if users migrated to a competitor tomorrow, how much would they lose?
5. **Efficient scale.** Market supports only one or two players; entry is value-destroying for all (regional monopolies, infrastructure networks).

## Franchise vs. Commodity Business

A franchise: (1) needed or desired, (2) thought by customers to have no close substitute, (3) not price-regulated. It sustains returns above the cost of capital and tolerates poor management. A commodity business: homogenous product, market-set prices, industry returns approximate the cost of capital.

Deciding test: if this company raised prices 10% while competitors held steady, would customers flee (commodity) or largely stay (franchise)?

Franchises are not eternal — newspapers once had impregnable franchises. Monitor the trend: sustained gross-margin improvement and market-share growth signal a widening moat; persistent margin decline, share loss, and "intensifying competition" language signal a narrowing one.

## Business Model Tests

- **Simple and understandable**: clear value chain, transparent revenue sources, trackable key variables.
- **Durable**: does roughly the same thing as 5–10 years ago.
- **Capital-efficient**: high returns without heavy reinvestment (See's Candies: $25M net assets, $400M+ pre-tax profit over twenty years).
- **Toll bridge test**: customers must pass through you.
- **Hypothetical competitor test**: with ample capital and talent, would you want to compete against it?

## Goodwill: Economic vs. Accounting

Accounting goodwill is the purchase premium on the balance sheet. Economic goodwill is the capitalized value of excess earning power — the ability to earn far above-market returns on net tangible assets. Accounting goodwill amortizes down; economic goodwill grows.

In analysis, ignore goodwill amortization charges. High goodwill plus high sustained ROE signals real economic goodwill; high goodwill plus mediocre ROE signals overpriced acquisitions.

## Durability

Durability beats scale — a small but durable advantage is worth more than a large fleeting one. A moat that must be constantly rebuilt is no moat.
